what_i_learned: |
  Today was dedicated to practicing our research presentation in preparation for the final research symposium. My team presented our complete presentation and received detailed feedback that helped us improve both the content and our delivery. Practicing in front of others reminded me that a strong presentation requires much more than simply understanding the research. It also requires confidence, organization, clear communication, and the ability to connect with the audience. As we rehearsed, I became more comfortable explaining our research on predictive stability versus fairness instability in clinical ECG classification. Each practice session helped me better understand how to communicate complex artificial intelligence concepts in a way that is accurate, engaging, and easier for people outside of computer science to understand. One of the most valuable parts of today was receiving feedback from Dr. Naja Mack after our presentation. She provided thoughtful suggestions about our presentation format, transitions between speakers, slide organization, and how we projected our voices while presenting. I appreciated that she focused not only on the scientific content but also on our presentation skills because both are equally important during a research symposium. Her observations helped us identify areas that our team had overlooked, including small formatting issues and opportunities to improve our delivery. After listening to her recommendations, we immediately revised our slides and practiced the updated presentation again. I learned that constructive feedback is an essential part of professional growth because it allows us to strengthen our work before presenting it to a larger audience.

blockers: |
  One challenge I experienced today was projecting my voice consistently throughout the presentation. Although I felt confident in my understanding of the research, I noticed that my voice became quieter whenever I felt nervous or focused too much on remembering my next point. Dr. Mack encouraged me to slow down, project my voice more clearly, and speak with confidence so that everyone in the audience could easily hear and understand my presentation. At first, it was difficult to adjust because speaking loudly in front of a group does not come naturally to me. However, after practicing multiple times and applying her feedback, I began to feel more comfortable. This experience reminded me that effective public speaking is a skill that develops through consistent practice rather than something people are simply born knowing how to do. Another challenge involved improving my overall presentation style instead of relying too heavily on my memorized script. I wanted my delivery to sound conversational and confident rather than rehearsed or robotic. This required me to focus on understanding the key ideas behind each slide instead of memorizing every sentence word for word. I also worked on maintaining better eye contact with the audience and allowing myself to pause naturally between important points. Although making these adjustments required additional practice, I noticed that my presentation became much smoother and more engaging. These challenges helped me recognize that becoming a stronger presenter is an ongoing process, and every opportunity to practice helps me continue developing this important professional skill.

reflection: |
  Reflecting on today, I feel excited and optimistic as we approach the final research symposium. Looking back over the past several weeks, I can clearly see how much I have grown as both a researcher and a presenter. At the beginning of the internship, I was unfamiliar with many of the artificial intelligence concepts we are now presenting with confidence. Through reading scientific literature, writing sections of our manuscript, coding, analyzing results, and preparing presentations, I have developed a much stronger understanding of our research and its significance. Today's rehearsal gave me confidence that our team is prepared to effectively communicate the importance of evaluating both predictive performance and fairness in healthcare AI. I am proud of the dedication and teamwork that brought us to this point. I also appreciate the honest and encouraging feedback we received today because it showed me that there is always room to improve, even when you feel prepared. Dr. Mack's suggestions helped me recognize that small improvements in voice projection, pacing, and presentation style can make a significant difference in how an audience receives a presentation. Instead of feeling discouraged by the feedback, I feel motivated to continue practicing and strengthening my public speaking abilities. I know that becoming a confident presenter will benefit me not only during this symposium but also throughout graduate school, future research conferences, and my career in healthcare. As the symposium approaches, I feel much more confident than I did just a few days ago, and I am genuinely excited to share our research with others. Overall, today's experience reminded me that preparation, constructive feedback, and continuous practice are the keys to becoming both a stronger researcher and a more effective communicator.
