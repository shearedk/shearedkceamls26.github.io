what_i_learned: |
  Today marked the final day of the NSEA Research Symposium, and it was one of the most rewarding experiences of my academic journey. I had the opportunity to present my research, "Predictive Stability vs. Fairness Instability in Clinical ECG Classification: A Multi-Run Analysis of Deep Learning Models," to faculty members, researchers, students, and guests. Standing in front of an audience and explaining months of work was both exciting and nerve-racking, but once I began speaking, I felt my confidence continue to grow. The audience appeared genuinely engaged throughout the presentation, and seeing people nod, ask thoughtful questions, and show interest in our findings reassured me that our research made an impact. I received positive feedback on both the quality of the research and the way it was presented, which made all of the preparation and practice worthwhile. This experience taught me that confidence is built through preparation, and that stepping outside of my comfort zone allows me to discover strengths that I did not know I possessed. Throughout today's symposium, I also learned how meaningful it is to share research with a broader scientific community. Listening to the presentations from the other research teams reminded me that every project, regardless of its discipline, contributes to solving important problems and advancing knowledge. One of the most exciting moments of the day was attending the awards ceremony, where the outstanding posters and elevator pitch videos were recognized. I was incredibly honored and grateful that our team's elevator pitch video was awarded first place. Winning this award made me realize that effective science communication is just as important as conducting strong research because discoveries only create impact when they can be clearly understood by others. Receiving this recognition was a proud moment for both my teammates and me because it reflected the creativity, dedication, and teamwork we invested throughout the summer. Today's experiences reinforced my passion for research and showed me that combining technical knowledge with effective communication can make a meaningful difference.

blockers: |
  Although today was an incredible success, I still experienced a few personal challenges before presenting. As I waited for my turn to speak, I naturally felt nervous because I wanted to represent my team and our months of research in the best way possible. I was concerned about speaking clearly, remembering my key points, and answering questions with confidence. However, once I began presenting, those feelings gradually disappeared as I focused on explaining the research rather than worrying about making mistakes. I realized that the countless hours of preparation, rehearsals, and constructive feedback had prepared me much better than I initially believed. This experience reminded me that nervousness is a normal part of public speaking and that confidence grows through experience rather than perfection. Another challenge was realizing how quickly this research experience has come to an end. Over the past several weeks, the SAIRI program became part of my daily routine, and I developed meaningful relationships with my mentors, teammates, and fellow researchers. Knowing that today marked the conclusion of the symposium made me reflect on how much I will miss collaborating with everyone and learning in such a supportive environment. At the same time, I recognized that this is not the end of my research journey but the beginning of many new opportunities. Instead of viewing the conclusion of the program as something to be sad about, I now see it as a foundation for future research experiences. That perspective has helped me transition from feeling bittersweet to feeling excited about what comes next.

reflection: |
  Reflecting on today, I can honestly say that this summer research experience has changed me in many ways. When I first arrived at SAIRI, I knew very little about deep learning, fairness evaluation, ECG classification, reproducibility, or explainability in artificial intelligence. Over the course of the program, I learned how to read scientific literature, contribute to a research manuscript, analyze machine learning models, present technical findings, and collaborate within a multidisciplinary research team. Most importantly, I gained confidence in my ability to conduct meaningful research and communicate it effectively to others. Presenting today showed me that I am capable of discussing complex scientific topics with confidence while remaining authentic and approachable. I leave this program with a much stronger belief in my own abilities than I had when the summer first began. I am incredibly grateful to everyone who made this experience possible, including my faculty mentor, graduate mentor, the SAIRI leadership team, and my fellow researchers. Their guidance, encouragement, and constructive feedback challenged me to grow academically, professionally, and personally throughout the internship. Winning first place in the elevator pitch competition was an unforgettable way to conclude the program, but the greatest reward was the knowledge, friendships, and confidence I gained along the way. This experience has strengthened my desire to continue participating in research throughout graduate school and my future career because I now understand how research has the power to improve healthcare and positively impact people's lives. I am leaving this program inspired to continue asking meaningful questions, pursuing new opportunities, and contributing to scientific discoveries that promote fairness, innovation, and better patient outcomes. Overall, today was not simply the end of an internship—it was the beginning of a lifelong commitment to research, learning, and professional growth.
